Major dispute between Somalia's aviation minister and the Civil Aviation Authority director

An administrative dispute between Somalia's Minister of Transport and Civil Aviation and the Director of the Somali Civil Aviation Authority, Ahmed Moallim, raises questions about the division of powers, institutional independence and who holds final responsibility for overseeing the country's civil aviation.

This story was published by Wartabiye TV24.11.2026 · 21:15 (EAT)

Mogadishu – Wartabiye TV | Report and Analysis

An administrative dispute between Somalia's Minister of Transport and Civil Aviation and the Director of the Somali Civil Aviation Authority, Ahmed Moallim, has raised questions about the division of powers, the independence of state institutions and who holds final responsibility for directing and overseeing the country's civil aviation.

The dispute is reported to be deepening after the director of the Civil Aviation Authority issued a memo on how the authority's offices and departments should handle orders and working contacts coming from the minister's office.

The memo has sparked a debate over the limits of the minister's and the director's powers, particularly when it comes to day-to-day management, technical decisions and the ministry's oversight authority.

This is not merely a personal disagreement between two officials. It touches on the smooth running of an institution that is vital to security, air traffic and Somalia's relationship with the international civil aviation system.

The director's memo and the ministry's position

According to information shared about the dispute, the memo issued by the director is described as restricting the orders and working instructions that reach the authority's departments from the minister's office.

If the memo broadly forbids the authority's departments from acting on any order from the ministry, an important legal question arises: does the director have the power to define, on his own, the limits of the official relationship between the authority and the ministry?

On the other hand, if the purpose of the memo is to protect administrative order and prevent conflicting instructions from reaching technical staff directly, it is important to understand why it was issued and which rules it is based on.

It is therefore essential to obtain the original memo and establish what it contains, to whom it was sent and under what legal authority it was issued.

Where does the dispute over powers lie?

The Civil Aviation Authority carries out technical and administrative work that requires expertise, operational independence and a clear system of work.

This includes aviation safety oversight, regulation of aircraft services, licences and permits, upholding security standards and coordinating flight-related services.

However, the authority's technical independence does not mean that it is independent of the entire system of government.

The responsible ministry has policy, guidance and oversight responsibilities tied to the rules that established the authority and to the minister's legal powers.

This is where the biggest question arises: where does the minister's oversight authority end, and where does the director's administrative and technical independence begin?

The answer must be sought in the law establishing the Civil Aviation Authority, the ministry's regulations and the official procedures of government administration.

Where the director could be wrong

If it is confirmed that the director's memo broadly restricts the ministry's legal powers, that step could create administrative confusion and a dispute over the hierarchy of state institutions.

The head of an agency has clear duties in managing its staff and operations, but the decisions he takes must be grounded in the rules and the powers delegated to him.

A dispute between two officials should not leave the authority's departments in a position where they do not know from whom to take official orders.

Likewise, if there are conflicting orders that are harming technical work, they should be resolved through a formal procedure rather than through one-sided decisions that could widen the dispute.

The minister's responsibility and the limits of interference

For his part, the Minister of Civil Aviation also bears responsibility for upholding oversight, accountability and cooperation among the institutions under his ministry.

The minister must make sure that the authority operates in line with government policy and the laws of the country.

However, oversight authority must not be interpreted as unlimited interference in technical decisions that should be taken by the authority's experts and management.

If there has been interference that does not comply with the rules, or orders that directly harm technical work, these matters should be resolved through legal and administrative channels.

The minister is therefore expected to clearly define his powers and to protect the authority's technical independence, without neglecting the oversight duties that rest with the ministry.

What impact could the dispute have on Somalia's civil aviation?

The Civil Aviation Authority is vital to Somalia's economy, security and international reputation.

Any dispute affecting senior management could cause delays in operational decisions, confusion among departments and weaker cooperation between officials.

In addition, the civil aviation sector works to international standards and procedures that require continuity, accountability and professional decision-making.

Somalia needs a civil aviation authority with stable management, staff who clearly know their duties and a system that does not depend on officials' personal disputes.

If this dispute drags on, it is important to ensure that it does not affect aviation services, passenger safety, airport operations or the authority's relations with international aviation bodies.

Questions that need answers

The dispute raises several questions that the two officials and the institutions concerned need to answer:

1. What exactly does the memo issued by the director of the Civil Aviation Authority say?

2. Does the director have legal authority to restrict orders and working instructions coming from the minister's office?

3. Does the minister have the power to intervene directly in all of the authority's administrative and technical decisions?

4. Has there been an earlier dispute over appointments, staff management or the authority's decision-making process?

5. What has the Ministry of Transport and Civil Aviation said about the memo and the director's action?

6. Will the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers become involved in resolving this dispute?

7. What guarantees that the dispute between the two officials will not affect essential civil aviation services?

Who holds final responsibility?

The biggest question now is not only who holds more power, but how the legal powers of each side have been defined.

The minister has policy and oversight duties grounded in law, while the director is responsible for managing and carrying out the tasks entrusted to the authority.

Neither side should use its powers to push aside the legal duties of the other.

If there is legal ambiguity, the matter should be referred to the state institutions with the authority to interpret the law and settle administrative disputes.

Conclusion: a power struggle or an administrative dispute?

The reported dispute between the Minister of Civil Aviation and the Director of the Civil Aviation Authority needs to be examined seriously, with a clear answer on what caused it and which rules each side is relying on.

Somalia does not need its key institutions to become arenas where officials compete for power. It needs administration built on law, accountability and cooperation.

It is important that both officials show administrative maturity and avoid any step that could harm the authority's work and public trust.

The final question Somalis are asking is this: will this dispute be resolved through dialogue and the law, or will it turn into a power struggle that affects the future of Somalia's civil aviation administration?
