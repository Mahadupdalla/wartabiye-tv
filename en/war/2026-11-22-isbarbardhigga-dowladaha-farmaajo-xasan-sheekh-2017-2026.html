Comparing the Record of Somalia's Two Governments: 2017–2022 and 2022–2026

The administrations of President Mohamed Abdullahi Farmaajo (2017–2022) and President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud (2022–2026) led the country through different phases, each with its own opportunities, political challenges, government priorities and results.

This story was published by Wartabiye TV22.11.2026 · 16:20 (EAT)

Political Analysis | WARTABIYE TV

Somalia has for a long time been in a process of rebuilding the state and strengthening national institutions, security, the economy and international relations. The two administrations of President Mohamed Abdullahi Farmaajo (2017–2022) and President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud (2022–2026) led the country through different phases, each with its own opportunities, political challenges, government priorities and results.

Comparing the two governments is important for understanding where Somalia has come from, where it stands today and what is still missing in building a capable state, stable security, a growing economy and a political system founded on agreement, cooperation and national unity.

Assessing the two periods should not be based on political support or opposition, but on verifiable performance, the policies implemented, the results achieved, the challenges that existed and the work that successive governments inherited from one another.

1. Diplomacy and international relations

Somalia's diplomacy plays a major role in rebuilding the state, securing international support, investment, security cooperation and protecting national interests. Both governments gave priority to international relations, although they differed in the political circumstances and opportunities they faced.

President Mohamed Abdullahi Farmaajo's government focused on restoring Somalia's relations with the international financial institutions, in particular the IMF and the World Bank, carrying out financial reforms that were part of the country's debt relief process.

Likewise, during that period Somalia was engaged in efforts to strengthen relations with countries in the region, international cooperation and the rebuilding of state capacity in the economic and political fields.

However, the political disputes between the federal government and some of the federal member states at times affected the coordination of national policy and the cohesion of the state.

President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's government focused on expanding international relations, strengthening security cooperation, relations with countries in the region and Somalia's membership of international organisations.

Somalia's membership of the East African Community (EAC) was an important step that opened new opportunities for the country in trade, the movement of people, the regional market, economic cooperation and relations with neighbouring countries.

The government also continued efforts to strengthen relations with countries that support Somalia, using diplomacy for security, the economy, investment and the country's development.

2. Somalia's debt relief and the role of the two governments

Somalia's debt relief is one of the greatest achievements in the economy and in rebuilding the state, but it was a long process in which different governments and international institutions took part.

In March 2020, President Farmaajo's government brought Somalia to the Decision Point of the HIPC debt relief programme. This step was important because it opened the way for the country to reach the final stage of debt relief.

Reforms of public financial management, taxation, domestic revenue, government transparency and the accountability system were part of the efforts that achieved that stage.

On 13 December 2023, President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's government brought Somalia to Completion Point, the final stage of HIPC debt relief. Somalia had nearly $4.5 billion of debt cancelled, which greatly reduced the debt burden the country was carrying.

Debt relief gave Somalia the opportunity to manage its budget better, invest in government services and plan development projects. It also raised the confidence of the international financial institutions and Somalia's chances of obtaining financing.

It is important to understand that debt relief was not the achievement of a single government. Farmaajo brought Somalia to the Decision Point during his government's term, while Hassan Sheikh completed Completion Point during his. The process was a national one in which successive governments, civil servants and international institutions took part.

3. Developing and improving the civil service

Civil servants are the backbone of a functioning state. A government cannot be effective unless it has professional staff, a regular payroll system, a clear hierarchy, accountability and administration based on knowledge and competence.

During President Farmaajo's government there were efforts to improve the civil service system, particularly in salaries, grades, staff registration and financial management. It was important to have a system ensuring that civil servants were officially registered, that their salaries were managed in an orderly way, and that recruitment and administration were improved.

These reforms were part of the efforts to strengthen the capacity of the federal government and reduce the errors in staff management and government spending.

Hassan Sheikh's government continued reforming public administration, with a focus on financial systems, transparency, accountability, staff management and strengthening institutions.

It is important to mention the civil service pay and grading policy (Pay and Grade Policy), approved in 2022, which was part of a broad reform of the civil service system.

Nevertheless, improving the civil service cannot be measured only by the policies approved. It is important to assess whether civil servants are paid regularly, whether recruitment is based on knowledge and competence, whether continuous training is provided, and whether government services become transparent and serve citizens well.

4. Rebuilding state institutions and statehood

Rebuilding state institutions is one of the hardest tasks facing Somalia. Since the collapse of the central government, the country has been in a process of building administrative institutions, the legal system, public finances, social services and the authority of the state within the country.

Farmaajo's government focused on reforming the financial system, domestic revenue, public administration and relations with international institutions. Those efforts were part of building a government system capable of managing revenue, paying expenditure, implementing government plans and having a financial system that can be trusted.

Hassan Sheikh's government continued the reform work, focusing on legislation, financial management, institutional reform, transparency and national development plans. Plans for transforming statehood, the economy and administration were also strengthened.

However, Somalia's state institutions still face challenges including limited working capacity, a lack of funds, corruption, uneven services and state authority that is not fully present in all areas of the country.

The success of rebuilding state institutions will depend on turning plans and laws into visible services from which citizens genuinely benefit.

5. Security and the fight against Al-Shabaab

Security has been, and remains, one of the biggest issues that every government ruling Somalia is expected to address. Al-Shabaab has long been an obstacle to statehood, the economy, the public and the stability of the country.

Between 2017–2022, Somalia continued building and training the armed forces, with support from international partners and the forces present in the country. Farmaajo's government faced continuous attacks, bombings, targeted killings and challenges related to the capacity of the forces, security coordination and the presence of the state in many areas.

Building the forces, improving training, paying salaries and developing security capacity were important issues worked on during that period. Nevertheless, during that time Al-Shabaab still had the ability to carry out attacks inside the country, which showed that the security fight is not limited to building forces alone, but requires a broad plan covering political, economic and social dimensions.

When Hassan Sheikh's government took office in 2022, it gave top priority to the fight against Al-Shabaab. A large military campaign was launched, involving government forces, local communities, local militias and the support of international partners.

The fighting focused particularly on the central regions, in an attempt to drive Al-Shabaab out of areas where it had been present for a long time and to weaken its military, financial and administrative power.

That campaign led to Al-Shabaab being driven out of some areas, and it showed the strength of cooperation between government forces and the public when they stand together. However, the fighting also faced challenges related to recapturing and holding areas, coordination of forces, logistics, operational support and Al-Shabaab's ability to return with attacks.

Assessing security is not only about how much territory is captured. It is also important that captured areas are held permanently, that government services are provided to the public, that the forces receive adequate salaries and equipment, and that district administrations are built.

Therefore, security is an area where both governments made efforts, but it remains the biggest challenge to Somalia's statehood.

6. The economy, domestic revenue and investment

Somalia's economy has long depended on trade, livestock, remittances from the diaspora, international aid and the private sector. Successive governments have tried to increase domestic revenue, improve tax administration, strengthen the financial system and create a good environment for investment.

Farmaajo's government focused on financial reform, increasing government revenue, regulating taxation and restoring Somalia's relations with the international financial institutions.

Those efforts were part of a plan intended to give the government additional economic capacity, so that it could manage expenditure, pay for services and implement development plans.

Hassan Sheikh's government benefited from debt relief while continuing economic reform, financial management, the banking system and investment plans. Debt relief created new opportunities for Somalia to obtain financing and build the confidence of financial institutions.

Likewise, joining the EAC can create new trade opportunities, if Somalia becomes able to grow domestic production, trade, services and infrastructure that connects markets.

Although there have been reforms and progress, the Somali people still face widespread unemployment, poverty, low purchasing power, a cost of living that weighs on families, an economy dependent on foreign aid, inadequate economic infrastructure and limited job opportunities.

A government's economic success cannot be measured by agreements and plans alone; it is measured by how the lives of citizens improve, jobs are created, household incomes rise and the country moves to a productive economy.

7. Economic infrastructure and social services

Economic infrastructure such as roads, ports, airports, electricity, water and telecommunications plays a major role in Somalia's development.

The governments of both periods faced a great need to rebuild the country's infrastructure, which affects trade, the movement of people, social services and urban development.

Under Farmaajo there were efforts to improve financial systems and support for development projects, while economic infrastructure still depended on domestic funding, donations and foreign support. The need for roads, government services, water, health and education remained very great, as the government faced limited funds and difficult security conditions.

Under Hassan Sheikh, plans for developing infrastructure and services continued, with priority given to economic reform, investment, international relations and national development plans.

However, there is still a great need for health services, education, roads, water, electricity, housing and job creation.

Progress in economic infrastructure should be assessed by the projects completed, the quality of services, the people who benefited and how the projects affected people's lives.

8. Federalism, political unity and relations with the federal member states

Political unity and relations between the federal government and the federal member states are among the issues that most affect Somalia's statehood. The federal system requires cooperation, power sharing, political agreements, consultation and respect between the federal government and the federal member states.

Under Farmaajo, relations between the federal government and the federal member states saw recurring political disputes, which affected cooperation, elections, security matters and political agreements. Those disputes at times made it harder to coordinate national policy, and there were differing views on the powers of the federal government, federal administration and how national issues should be handled.

Hassan Sheikh's government tried to strengthen political consultation, using the National Consultative Council and meetings between the federal government and the leaders of the federal member states. Those meetings were important for discussing security, politics, elections, federalism and statehood plans.

Nevertheless, political disputes still existed, which shows that Somalia has not yet reached a full agreement on all issues of power sharing and political structure.

There is a need to strengthen regular consultation, clarify the powers of the federal government and the federal member states, protect political agreements, resolve disputes through dialogue, and put the interests of the public ahead of political disputes.

Political unity is important for security, the economy, statehood and international relations.

9. Comparing the record of the two governments

Looking at the different aspects of statehood, the two administrations each had their own achievements and challenges.

Farmaajo's government gave particular importance during its term to financial reform, relations with international institutions, reaching the first stage of debt relief, regulating the civil service and building government systems.

Hassan Sheikh's government achieved during its term the completion of debt relief, EAC membership, a large military campaign against Al-Shabaab, continued financial reform and stronger international relations.

Both governments worked in different circumstances, and there was work that each government started or continued whose results appeared at a later time.

Therefore, it is important that achievements are recognised fairly, distinguishing the work each government did directly, the work that previous governments had started and the support of international institutions.

10. What is still missing for Somalia?

Beyond the comparison of the two presidents, there are major problems that still need a national solution.

The country's security needs a sustained plan to weaken Al-Shabaab, strengthen the forces and ensure that liberated areas receive administration and government services.

Somalia's economy needs to move beyond dependence on aid and consumption, towards a productive economy based on agriculture, livestock, fisheries, industry, trade and energy.

Somali youth need jobs, skills, training, business opportunities and financing. Widespread unemployment affects people's lives and the stability of the country.

There is also a need for functioning state institutions, a reliable legal system, transparency, fighting corruption, accountability and government services that reach the public.

Political disputes and federal disagreements need solutions based on dialogue and agreement, to protect the country from division and political setbacks.

11. Rating the two governments: 60% and 40%

When making a political assessment, the scores given to presidents depend on the criteria used and how they are weighted.

If one wants to give Farmaajo 60/100 and Hassan Sheikh 40/100, it must be made clear what those scores are based on: security, the economy, diplomacy, federalism, the civil service, social services or overall performance.

Scores of that kind are an opinion or an editorial assessment, and cannot be official fact unless they are based on data and a clear method of measurement.

A fair assessment requires that the two governments are measured by the same criteria, looking at the budgets they managed, the domestic revenue collected, the projects started and those completed, the areas secured, the social services improved, the jobs created, the political agreements reached and the progress of state institutions.

This approach helps make the assessment free of bias, party support or political interests, and based on data and evidence.

12. Conclusion

The comparison of the governments of President Mohamed Abdullahi Farmaajo and President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud shows that Somalia has been in a process of state rebuilding in which different administrations have taken part.

Farmaajo's government gave particular importance during its term to financial reform, relations with international institutions, reaching the first stage of debt relief and developing government administrative systems.

Hassan Sheikh's government completed Somalia's debt relief during its term, strengthened international relations, brought Somalia into the EAC and carried out a large military campaign against Al-Shabaab, while continuing state and financial reforms.

Both governments had achievements, challenges and unfinished work. There is progress to which each government contributed, and processes that earlier governments began and later governments continued.

Therefore, the biggest question is not only which government was better than the other, but which government effectively turned its plans and promises into visible results that changed the lives of the Somali people?

The answer to that question requires data, evidence, independent assessment and measuring the two administrations by the same criteria.

Somalia needs a government with stable security, a growing economy, capable institutions, job creation, social services and political unity. The country's progress should not depend on a single president, but must become a national plan that every government continues, that the public benefits from and that future generations develop.
