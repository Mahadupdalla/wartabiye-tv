Farmaajo welcomes Museveni's visit and sends a political message to Somalia's leadership

Former President Mohamed Abdullahi Farmaajo has welcomed Ugandan President Yoweri Museveni's expected visit to Mogadishu and urged Somalia's federal leadership to resolve political disputes through dialogue and the Constitution.

This story was published by Wartabiye TV24.11.2026 · 17:40 (EAT)

POLITICAL ANALYSIS | MOGADISHU

Former Somali President Mohamed Abdullahi Farmaajo has welcomed the expected visit of Ugandan President Yoweri Kaguta Museveni to Mogadishu, using the occasion to send a political message to the leadership of Somalia's Federal Government about the country's political situation, dialogue and the importance of resolving existing disputes through a constitutional process.

In a post on his X (Twitter) account, Farmaajo indicated that the Ugandan President's visit comes at a time when the country faces political disputes that require genuine dialogue, mutual understanding and a solution that all parties involved in politics can accept.

The former president's message touches in particular on the Constitution, the legality of political decisions and the dispute over the system of governance, issues that have recently become central to Somalia's political debate.

1. Farmaajo welcomes Museveni's visit

Farmaajo's welcome of President Museveni's visit comes at a time when relations between Somalia and Uganda carry weight in security, diplomacy and regional politics.

Uganda has long been part of the security support for Somalia, with its troops playing a role in peacekeeping operations and the fight against Al-Shabaab. For that reason, Museveni's visit to Mogadishu, when it takes place, could carry significance beyond relations between the two governments, especially in light of Somalia's internal political situation.

In welcoming the visit, Farmaajo highlighted the importance of relations between Somalia and Uganda, but his political message also appears to stress the need for the country to move past the current political disputes and to find a process of dialogue to resolve matters of national importance.

2. The Constitution and Supreme Court rulings cited by Farmaajo

The main point that can be drawn from Farmaajo's message is his emphasis on the importance of the Constitution and the rulings of judicial institutions, especially where there are political disputes over the terms of government institutions and the electoral process.

In his post, Farmaajo referred to the country's Constitution and to the Supreme Court ruling he cited, indicating that Somalia's political disputes must be addressed through a legal and constitutional process.

This touches in particular on the dispute over the terms of government institutions, amendments to the Constitution and the differing views politicians hold on how the country's elections should be handled.

The former president stresses in his message that political solutions cannot be limited to one-sided decisions, but require a legal process, consultation and respect for constitutional institutions.

3. The political message to the government's leadership

Farmaajo's message can be read as a call for the Federal Government's leadership to prioritise genuine political dialogue capable of resolving disputes between the government, the opposition and other political actors in the country.

By citing the Constitution, the Supreme Court and the need for dialogue, the former president revives the debate on how political disputes are managed and on who should have a say in decisions of national importance.

This debate comes at a time when there are differing views in Somalia on the electoral process, the powers of government institutions and the country's political future.

Farmaajo's message also shows that opposition politicians and former leaders want these issues to be discussed openly, instead of remaining in dispute and political deadlock.

4. Museveni and Uganda's role in Somali politics

President Yoweri Kaguta Museveni and the Ugandan government have long had close ties with Somalia, especially in security, troop support and efforts to stabilise the country.

Museveni has also previously played a role in political efforts in Somalia, and Uganda's role in the 2011 Kampala Accord is especially remembered; that agreement resolved a political dispute within the leadership of the then Transitional Federal Government.

That history is why any visit by Museveni to Somalia draws particular attention from politicians, political analysts and Somali society, especially when the country faces political disputes that need a solution.

However, Museveni's invitation to Mogadishu and his acceptance of the visit do not on their own show that he will play a political mediation role in Somalia's internal dispute. That would require an agreement or an official confirmation from both sides.

5. Why Museveni's visit is a political opportunity

Museveni's visit to Mogadishu could be an opportunity for the leaders of Somalia and Uganda to discuss bilateral relations, security cooperation, the regional situation and Somalia's political affairs.

It is also possible that the visit leads to meetings and talks on political stability, as Somalia currently faces disputes over elections, the Constitution and relations between the Federal Government and other political actors.

Farmaajo, in welcoming the visit, indicates in his message that diplomatic opportunities can be used to encourage dialogue and find solutions to disputes, with respect for the country's law and Constitution.

However, the visit has been officially confirmed as relating to bilateral relations, while anything concerning political mediation or a solution to the internal dispute still requires further confirmation.

6. What can be expected from Museveni's visit to Mogadishu?

President Museveni has accepted President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud's invitation to visit Somalia, with the date of the trip to be agreed through diplomatic channels.

The visit is expected to focus on relations between Somalia and Uganda, security cooperation, the regional situation and matters of mutual interest to the two governments.

On Somalia's internal politics, some may see the visit as an opportunity to encourage dialogue and understanding, while others have no evidence yet that Uganda will be part of resolving Somalia's political disputes.

The significance of the visit will therefore depend on the official agenda of the two governments, the meetings held and the outcomes when Museveni comes to Mogadishu.

Conclusion

Mohamed Abdullahi Farmaajo's message revives the debate on political dialogue, respect for the Constitution and the importance of resolving Somalia's disputes through a legal and inclusive process.

His welcome of Museveni's visit also shows the importance of relations between Somalia and Uganda, and how diplomatic visits can become opportunities for leaders and politicians to discuss the country's political situation.

The question that remains is: Will Museveni's visit to Mogadishu be limited to strengthening bilateral relations and security cooperation, or will it become an opportunity to encourage political dialogue leading to a solution to Somalia's disputes?

The answer will be known when the visit takes place, the agenda of the meetings is announced and the positions of both sides are officially made public.
