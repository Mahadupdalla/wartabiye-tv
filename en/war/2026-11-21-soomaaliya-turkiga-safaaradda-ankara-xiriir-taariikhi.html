Somalia and Türkiye: A Historic Relationship Spanning Decades as the Ankara Embassy Enters a New Phase

The diplomatic relationship between Somalia and Türkiye rests on a long history, friendship, mutual trust and strategic interests that go back several decades.

This story was published by Wartabiye TV21.11.2026 · 13:35 (EAT)

Diplomatic Commentary | Wartabiye TV

The diplomatic relationship between Somalia and Türkiye rests on a long history, friendship, mutual trust and strategic interests that go back several decades. The relationship between the two countries is not limited to the cooperation visible today; it has a historical foundation that reflects earlier ties, the rebuilding of the Somali state and the two sides' cooperation in politics, security, the economy and social development.

The latest progress on the construction of the new premises of the Somali Embassy in Ankara is bringing the importance of that relationship back to the fore, showing that Somalia is continuing to strengthen its diplomatic presence, its international representation and its ties with its strategic partners.

A long-standing diplomatic relationship

Somalia and Türkiye have diplomatic relations that go back a long way, formally established on 9 February 1966. At the time Somalia was in the early stages of statehood, making a strong effort to build national institutions, international relations and to consolidate its political independence.

Relations between the two countries continued to grow in the years that followed, shaped by political cooperation, government-to-government ties and the shared interests of both sides in the Horn of Africa region and the Islamic world in general.

The opening of the Turkish Embassy in Mogadishu in 1979 was an important step that showed the level the relationship between the two governments had reached. The embassy symbolised a formal and growing relationship, with both countries maintaining diplomatic representation and government cooperation.

The role of Somalia's central government in building foreign relations

Somalia's central government, particularly during the period led by President Mohamed Siad Barre, was one of the important phases in the history of the country's diplomacy. Somalia at that time had state institutions, embassies, international relations and representation abroad that formed part of national policy.

The Revolutionary Government made efforts to build relations with various countries in Africa, Asia, the Middle East and Europe, with foreign relations seen as part of state power and the protection of Somalia's interests.

The opening of the Turkish Embassy in Mogadishu in 1979 was among the events that reflected the diplomatic relations Somalia had with friendly countries. Embassies were not only centres for managing relations between governments; they were also places where agreements, cooperation, trade relations and national representation were carried out.

That history shows that the relationship between Somalia and Türkiye has a foundation that predates today's political circumstances, and that it has gone through different phases of statehood and diplomacy.

The collapse of the state and the suspension of diplomatic relations

The collapse of Somalia's central government in 1991 had a major impact on all national institutions, including embassies, foreign relations, government services and Somalia's representation in the world.

That situation led many diplomatic relations to be suspended or to face major obstacles. The Turkish Embassy in Mogadishu was also closed in 1991, as the country entered a very difficult security, political and humanitarian situation.

Nevertheless, the relationship between Somalia and Türkiye did not disappear altogether. Türkiye continued to take an interest in Somalia's situation and in efforts to support peace, stability and the rebuilding of the country.

The closure of embassies and the collapse of state institutions was one of the most visible effects of the collapse of the state, showing how important it is to have a functioning government, permanent diplomatic relations and institutions capable of representing the country.

Erdoğan's 2011 visit and the revival of the relationship

The historic visit of Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, then Prime Minister of Türkiye, to Mogadishu on 19 August 2011 became an event that greatly shaped relations between the two countries.

The visit came at a time when Somalia was facing a severe drought, famine, displacement and a very difficult humanitarian situation. The arrival of Erdoğan and his delegation at that time carried both a political and a humanitarian message, showing that Türkiye was ready to stand by Somalia.

That visit played a major role in reviving relations between the two countries and paved the way for the expansion of Türkiye's support to Somalia, particularly in the areas of:

• Humanitarian aid and emergency relief.

• Health and social services.

• Education and workforce training.

• Economic infrastructure and road construction.

• Development of state institutions.

• Security and defence cooperation.

The Turkish Embassy in Mogadishu was reopened on 1 November 2011, another important step in restoring diplomatic relations between the two countries.

The 2011 visit thus became one of the most important events in Somalia–Türkiye relations in the modern era, greatly changing the level of cooperation between the two sides.

The Somali Embassy in Ankara and the agreement to build new premises

The current progress on the new premises of the Embassy of the Federal Republic of Somalia in Ankara has diplomatic, state-building and national symbolic significance.

On 6 July 2022, Somalia and Türkiye signed an agreement in Ankara on support for the construction of the Somali Embassy premises. The agreement is part of cooperation between the two governments and Türkiye's support for Somalia in diplomacy and institution-building.

The land allocated for the new Somali Embassy premises is located in the İncek area of Ankara and is reported to measure 4,918 square metres.

The new decision on implementing the agreement, approved on 20 November 2026, is important for moving the project into a new phase of implementation.

If the project is fully carried out, the new premises will give Somalia a formal diplomatic site suited to the needs of the embassy, services for Somali citizens, relations with the Turkish government and the diplomatic work of both countries.

What does a new embassy building mean for Somalia?

Building a new embassy is not just a construction project; it is part of state capacity and a country's presence in the world.

An embassy plays an important role in protecting citizens' interests, political relations, promoting trade, attracting investment, educational cooperation and strengthening ties between two governments.

Somalia, which has long been working to rebuild its state institutions, needs diplomatic missions able to carry out their duties well and to represent the country in places that matter for global politics and the economy.

Ankara is Türkiye's political capital, home to the most important government institutions, ministries, decision-making centres and the embassies of countries around the world. A new Somali facility in Ankara can therefore carry more significance than an ordinary office building.

It can become a place to strengthen political relations, develop trade cooperation and protect the interests of Somali citizens in Türkiye.

Türkiye and Somalia: Cooperation beyond ordinary diplomatic ties

Relations between Somalia and Türkiye today extend across many areas that are important for statehood and the country's development.

The two governments cooperate on security, military training, education, health, economic infrastructure, trade and the development of social services. Türkiye has carried out various projects in Somalia related to health services, education, transport and key infrastructure.

That cooperation creates opportunities for Somalia to strengthen state institutions, improve social services and broaden its relations with other countries.

For Türkiye, Somalia has strategic importance in the Horn of Africa, on important waterways, as a link between Africa and the Middle East, and for political and economic cooperation in the region.

The two countries share interests in regional stability, the protection of sovereignty, economic development and the expansion of international cooperation.

Economic and investment opportunities in the relationship

Strong diplomatic relations can be a foundation for growing trade, investment and cooperation between private companies.

Somalia needs investment in fisheries, agriculture, energy, industry, construction, transport and services. Türkiye has experience in industry, construction, services, manufacturing and trade, which can create cooperation opportunities that benefit both sides.

If diplomatic relations are turned into effective economic cooperation, it is possible to create jobs, expand trade, develop the skills of Somali workers and increase investment inside the country.

However, those opportunities require a clear plan, transparent agreements, the protection of national interests and government systems that support lasting investment.

The legacy of statehood and diplomacy: Yesterday, today and tomorrow

Looking at the history of relations between Somalia and Türkiye, it is clear that the diplomatic relationship has gone through different phases: the establishment of relations in 1966, the opening of the Turkish embassy in Mogadishu in 1979, the suspension that followed the collapse of the state in 1991, and the revival of the relationship in 2011.

These phases reflect the changes Somalia has gone through and the ability of international relations to play a role in rebuilding the state.

Somalia's central government has a historical chapter worth learning from in institution-building, foreign relations and national representation. But the priority today is to turn that legacy into modern institutions, lasting diplomatic relations and cooperation that directly benefits the Somali people.

Statehood is not limited to the history that has been lived; it rests on the capacity today to protect the nation, provide social services, maintain international relations and plan for the future.

Conclusion: The Ankara embassy as a possible symbol of a new chapter

Progress on the new premises of the Somali Embassy in Ankara can be an important step in strengthening the historic relationship between Somalia and Türkiye.

The project carries a meaning beyond the construction of a diplomatic facility. It can become a symbol of Somalia's presence in the world, the development of national representation, the protection of citizens' interests and the further consolidation of the strategic relationship between the two governments.

The history of Somalia–Türkiye relations shows that the friendship between the two countries has passed through different times, but that it still holds great opportunities that can be developed further in the future.

The government that built diplomatic relations yesterday, the government strengthening cooperation today, and the generation building national institutions tomorrow — all are part of the chain of Somalia's statehood, diplomacy and future.

The Ankara embassy may be a new building, but the meaning it carries is about history, independence, national representation and Somalia's aspirations for the future.
